Sorbet, Gelato & Granita

They all live in the freezer, but these frozen treats are made in different ways and feel very different on the spoon.

Sorbet

Fruit purée or juice, sugar and water, with no dairy. It is churned while freezing so the ice crystals stay tiny, giving a smooth, intensely fruity scoop. Because there is no fat, the sugar balance decides how soft it is.

Gelato

The Italian style of frozen dessert.

  • Usually made with more milk and less cream than ice cream.
  • Churned slowly, so less air is whipped in.
  • Served slightly warmer, so it feels dense and silky.

Sherbet

Sits between sorbet and ice cream: a fruit base with a small amount of milk or cream. That little bit of dairy makes it creamier than sorbet while keeping a bright fruit flavour.

Granita

A Sicilian classic made without churning. The mix is frozen in a shallow tray and scraped with a fork every half hour, forming light, crunchy flakes of ice. It is wonderfully simple and needs no equipment at all.

Which to make?

Choose sorbet for pure fruit flavour, gelato when you want richness, sherbet for something in between and granita when you have no machine and want a refreshing, icy treat on a hot day.
