Balance a sorbet

guide · 6 min read

Sorbet Without a Machine

An ice cream machine helps, but it is not essential. With a fork, a blender or a bag of frozen fruit, you can make sorbet that is smooth and full of flavour.

Get the balance right first

Without churning, sugar does even more of the work. Aim for a mix that is about a quarter sugar by weight, including the natural sugar in the fruit. Our sugar balance calculator does the arithmetic for you.

The fork method

Pour the mix into a shallow metal tray and freeze.

  • Every 30 minutes, scrape and stir with a fork.
  • Repeat four or five times as it firms up.
  • The more often you stir, the smoother it gets.

The blender method

Freeze the mix solid in an ice cube tray. Blend the cubes in a strong blender or food processor until smooth and creamy, then pack into a container and freeze for an hour before scooping.

Frozen fruit whip

For an instant version, blend frozen fruit with a spoonful of honey or syrup and a splash of juice. Mango, banana and berries work especially well because they are naturally thick and sweet.

Storing and serving

Keep sorbet in a shallow, covered container with baking paper pressed onto the surface to stop ice crystals forming. Eat it within a week or two for the best texture, and let it stand for a few minutes before scooping.

little tip

A tablespoon of liquid glucose or honey in place of some sugar keeps no-machine sorbet softer for longer.
